Everything below comes from WebSearch result snippets and summaries. Search summaries can misstate things, so check a URL before relying on a figure. ## Q1. Inventory: name, city, publisher, language, frequency, format, reach, active 2026, finance pages, contact route

### Takeaway
The UK Gujarati press is effectively a two-publisher market, both based in London. Asian Media Group (AMG) publishes Garavi Gujarat (founded 1968). Asian Business Publications Ltd (ABPL) publishes Gujarat Samachar UK (London edition since 1972) and its English sister Asian Voice. Both were still publishing weekly in 2026. Beyond them, the only titles are community or caste association publications: Oshwal News (Oshwal Association of the UK, Jain, members' hard copy, about two issues a year) and Navnat Darpan (Navnat Vanik Association newsletter). I found no Gujarati-language newspaper in Leicester, Portugal, the Netherlands or Antwerp/Belgium.

## Note: UK financial promotion rules for a foreign (Indian) adviser writing columns or placing ads

- FSMA s.21 bars communicating an invitation or inducement to engage in investment activity in the course of business unless the communicator is FCA-authorised or the content is approved by an authorised person. Breach is a criminal offence (s.25: up to 2 years' imprisonment and/or an unlimited fine). VERIFIED: [FD Capital s.21 guide](https://www.fdcapital.co.uk/section-21-financial-promotions-guide/)
- When an authorised firm approves a promotion for an overseas person, FCA COBS 4.9 requires the promotion to name the approving firm and to explain that UK regulatory protections and compensation may not apply. VERIFIED: [FCA Handbook COBS 4.9](https://www.handbook.fca.org.uk/handbook/COBS/4/9.html)
- Regulators have acted against overseas issuers and UK approvers: the Atlantic Law case involved 50 promotions approved for four unauthorised overseas companies targeting UK retail customers. VERIFIED: [Atlantic Law LLP v FSA](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/5752d9fe40f0b64328000030/Atlantic_Law_LLP_v_FSA.pdf)
- INFERENCE: an Indian adviser's paid ad, or a column that promotes the adviser's own investment services to UK readers, would likely be a financial promotion needing an FCA-authorised approver. Purely educational, non-promotional commentary is lower risk. Get UK legal advice. Newspaper-specific guidance was not found.
