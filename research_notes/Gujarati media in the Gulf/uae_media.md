# Gujarati-language and Gujarati-serving media in the UAE (as of 11 Oct 2026)

Method note (applies to everything below): Research ran on 11 Oct 2026 using about 35 WebSearch queries in standard and extended modes, including Gujarati-script queries ("ગુજરાતી દુબઈ સમાચાર", "ગુજરાતી સમાજ દુબઈ"). Every WebFetch attempt failed with DNS errors (khaleejtimes.com, gujaratisamajuae.ae, a1omads.com). So no page was read in full. Claims tagged VERIFIED rest on search-engine snippets or summaries of the cited URL, not on a full page read. INFERENCE marks my reasoning. Absence findings mean "not found in indexed search results", which is strong evidence but not proof.

## Q1. Outlet inventory: name, city, format, frequency, language, reach, owner, 2025-26 activity, contact route

### Takeaway
No independent Gujarati-language outlet (newspaper, magazine, radio, TV or news site) based in the UAE could be verified. The working "Gujarati media" layer is made up of: (a) community-organisation channels, meaning the Gujarati Samaj UAE website, monthly newsletter, app and WhatsApp/social groups, plus Gujarati Samaj Abu Dhabi and caste samaj sites; (b) Instagram community or influencer accounts such as "Ame Gujarati In UAE"; (c) English-language UAE mainstream media (Gulf News, Khaleej Times, Zawya/Observer Dubai press-release syndication) that cover Gujarati community stories in English; (d) Gujarat-based Gujarati outlets (Zee 24 Kalak, TV9 Gujarati, VTV, Divya Bhaskar, Gujarat Samachar, Chitralekha), which UAE Gujaratis read digitally.

## Q2. Is any Gujarati newspaper printed in the UAE today? Any historical ones?

### Takeaway
No. A 2024 academic study reported by Gulf Today explicitly notes the absence of Gujarati (and Hindi, Telugu, Marathi) ethnic newspapers in the UAE. The only Indian-language papers printed there are Malayalam titles and one Tamil paper (Daily Thanthi). I found no historical Gujarati newspaper published in the UAE either.

### Cited Findings
- The study by KT Abdurabb found that Malayala Manorama, Gulf Madhyamam, Siraj and Mathrubhumi print seven days a week in the UAE. It notes "the absence of similar ethnic newspapers in other Indian languages such as Hindi, Telugu, Marathi or Gujarati in the UAE, even though their speakers are numerous", with Tamil's eight-page Daily Thanthi as the exception. VERIFIED (Oct 2024) — [Gulf Today](https://www.gulftoday.ae/culture/2024/10/10/pioneering-study-on-malayalam-newspapers-in-the-uae)
- Wikipedia's list of UAE newspapers contains Arabic, English and Malayalam titles and no Gujarati ones — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_newspapers_in_the_United_Arab_Emirates)
- Daily Thanthi's Dubai edition was a launch of a print edition of an Indian regional daily in the UAE, the model Gujarati papers have not followed — [Khaleej Times](https://www.khaleejtimes.com/uae/leading-tamil-newspaper-now-available-in-uae?amp=1)
- Searches for a historical Gujarati newspaper in the Gulf or UAE returned only the history of Gujarati papers in India (Bombay Samachar, 1822) and UAE Arabic papers (Al Khaleej, 1970) — [Wikiwand: Media in Gujarati](https://www.wikiwand.com/en/articles/Media_in_Gujarati_language); [Dar Al Khaleej](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dar_Al_Khaleej)

### Inferences
- Whether India-printed Gujarati papers or Chitralekha are air-freighted to UAE newsstands is unverified. INFERENCE: if they are, the volume is small. Chitralekha's international print subscription is the only documented overseas print route.

### Gaps
- I could not search pre-internet archives, such as 1970s-90s Dubai cyclostyled Gujarati bulletins. A historical Gujarati newsletter by Samaj bodies (Abu Dhabi Samaj, founded 1977; Parajiya Soni, 1974) is plausible but undocumented online.
