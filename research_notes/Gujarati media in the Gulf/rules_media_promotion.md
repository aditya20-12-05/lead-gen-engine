## Objective: per-country summary of (a) media licensing and content rules and (b) financial promotion rules, plus the Indian (SEBI) side

### Takeaway
Every GCC state treats promoting financial services or products to its residents as a licensed activity, and the test is usually where the audience is, not where the promoter sits. The UAE (from 2025-26) and Kuwait (law in force April 2027) have also added media-law permits for anyone who advertises to local audiences. A foreign unlicensed firm running paid product ads or seminars aimed at Gulf residents faces real risk everywhere. Purely educational writing on Indian tax, FEMA or inheritance in a third-party publication is lower-risk, but no country gives a clean written safe harbour for it. On the Indian side, the current SEBI rules require prior approval for IA advertisements, and SEBI has said it will move to post-issue reporting with an exemption for educational content. The final circular was not confirmed as of October 2026.

Celebrities are barred without SEBI written approval, and the SEBI standard risk warning must be used verbatim. Registered name and ARN must appear on social media (enforced from 1 May 2026). - VERIFIED: SEBI required intermediaries' identities to be verified before they advertise on social media (from March 2025). Meta's stricter enforcement from July 2025 requires a SEBI registration number. [Mondaq 2026](https://webiis10.mondaq.com/india/securities/1759228/sebis-new-digital-compliance-rules-what-investment-advisers-must-know-in-2026)

### Inferences
- INFERENCE: A Gujarati community newsletter or radio programme is "media", so the publisher or station (not the guest columnist) normally carries the media-licence obligation. That changes when the contributor is paid to promote, or promotes their own firm. In the UAE that triggers the Advertiser Permit analysis, and in Kuwait from April 2027 the promoter licence.
- INFERENCE: Under the SEBI Code as it stands (prior approval), a SELEQT column carrying the firm's name, SEBI registration or a call to action would likely count as an "advertisement" needing prior approval, even in Gulf media. If the Common Advertisement Code is finalised as proposed, pure education with no promotion of SELEQT's services may fall outside it.
- INFERENCE: Any paid ad by SELEQT in Gulf media that invites residents to buy Indian mutual funds or advisory services would almost certainly fall within each GCC "promotion"/"invitation" prohibition unless an exemption applies (for example UAE reverse solicitation or professional-investor routes). The safer route is to partner with a locally licensed entity.

### Gaps
- Primary statute texts (UAE CMA Resolution 10/R.M/2025, Media Council content standards, Kuwait Decree-Law 102/2026, Oman executive regulations, QCB law cross-border marketing section) could not be read because WebFetch failed.
- No source covered CBUAE promotion rules for foreign firms, Saudi CMA finfluencer rules, or FSRA finfluencer guidance.
- Whether Qatar's comprehensive media law was ever enacted remains unconfirmed.
- The final text and effective date of SEBI's Common Advertisement Code remain unconfirmed.

## Is a purely educational column (Indian tax, FEMA, inheritance) with no product promotion treated differently from a financial promotion in each country?

### Takeaway
In practice yes, but mostly through definitions rather than express safe harbours. The UAE finfluencer regime targets "financial recommendations", Saudi and Kuwait target securities business and invitations, and the DFSA targets "financial promotions". General education on Indian tax, FEMA or inheritance that names no product and makes no offer is generally outside these definitions. No regulator text I found exempts "educational content" expressly. SEBI's proposed Common Advertisement Code would exempt pure education, but that is not confirmed final.

### Cited Findings
- UAE: commentators say general explanation of how products work is not a regulated recommendation, while buy/sell/hold calls are. [UpperSetup](https://uppersetup.com/en/blog/the-uae-finfluencer-license-2026-registering-financial). The listed exclusions under Resolution 10/R.M/2025 do not include education, and they are interpreted narrowly. [Chambers](https://chambers.com/articles/new-uae-law-regulates-finfluencers-and-financial-information-provided-on-social-media) (VERIFIED, secondary)
- UAE: the Advertiser Permit exemption for educational or awareness content is reported only for under-18s posting non-commercial content. [Gulf News](https://gulfnews.com/uae/new-uae-law-advertiser-permit-now-mandatory-for-influencers-and-creators-for-social-media-1.500427938) (VERIFIED)
- UAE: the finfluencer regime reportedly extends to public speaking, seminars and TV appearances that promote investment content relevant to the UAE. [Hadef](https://hadefpartners.com/news-insights/insights/regulating-influence-the-uae-s-new-compliance-regime-for-finfluencers/) (VERIFIED, secondary)
- Saudi Arabia: a Mawthooq licence is needed for revenue-earning promotional content about products or services offered in the Kingdom. [Saudipedia](https://saudipedia.com/en/what-is-the-mawthooq-license) (VERIFIED)
- India: the 29 January 2025 SEBI clarification allows association with pure investor-education entities that give no advice, make no return claims and use price data at least 3 months old. [KS&K](https://ksandk.com/newsletter/sebi-unregistered-investment-claim-makers/) (VERIFIED)
- India: under the proposed Common Advertisement Code, purely educational or awareness content without promotion of the entity's products would fall outside the code. [Vinod Kothari](https://vinodkothari.com/2026/06/sebis-new-advertisement-code-dil-khol-ke-advertise-kar/) (VERIFIED, proposal)

### Inferences
- INFERENCE: Saudi Mawthooq is aimed at paid promotion, so an unpaid Indian tax or FEMA column in a community newsletter would likely not need it. A column that promotes SELEQT's services to Saudi residents would be promotion of a service not licensed in the Kingdom.
- INFERENCE: An unpaid column in Kuwait could still fall under Decree-Law 102/2026 (in force April 2027) if it amounts to "promotion" of SELEQT, because the law covers unpaid promotion. Pure education without self-promotion is less likely to be caught, depending on the executive regulations.
- INFERENCE: The risk rises with a byline that carries a firm name, logo, contact or call-to-action, and with any mention of specific Indian mutual funds or "returns". The SEBI rules (no claims of returns, no superlatives) push in the same direction.

### Gaps
- No Qatar, Oman, Bahrain or Kuwait source addressed an educational exemption explicitly.

## Do paid ads by a foreign unlicensed financial firm in local Gulf media breach local rules?

### Takeaway
Very likely yes, if the ad promotes financial products or advisory or distribution services to residents. UAE onshore, DIFC, Saudi Arabia, Kuwait and Bahrain all have audience-based prohibitions or regulator powers over promotion by unlicensed persons. Oman and Qatar regulators warn against and pursue unlicensed promoters. Media-law permits come on top of this in the UAE (Advertiser Permit) and, from 2027, Kuwait.

### Cited Findings
- UAE: foreign products may not be promoted except by a licensed promoter, subject to exemptions. [Al Tamimi](https://tamimi.com/news/promotion-of-foreign-funds-in-the-uae) (VERIFIED)
- DIFC: Financial Promotions "in or from the DIFC" are restricted to authorised or exempt persons. [DFSA](https://dfsa.ae/download_file/744/439) (VERIFIED)
- Saudi Arabia: securities business "with or for a person in Saudi Arabia" needs a licence. [Clifford Chance](https://www.cliffordchance.com/content/dam/cliffordchance/briefings/2012/09/cma-warns-unlicensed-entities-against-conducting-securities-business-in-the-kingdom-of-saudi-arabia.pdf). Foreign funds can only be offered via CMA-licensed distributors. [Middle East Briefing](https://www.middleeastbriefing.com/news/?p=4571) (VERIFIED)
- Kuwait: no invitation or announcement of securities may be sent to a person in Kuwait unless by a licensed person. A 2024 CMA disciplinary resolution was issued for marketing foreign CIS units without a licence. [Al Tamimi](https://tamimi.com/law-update-articles/the-marketing-of-foreign-funds-in-kuwait); [KFH disclosure, Aug 2024](https://kfh.com/dam/jcr:22ff3876-71b0-4185-bd28-68214dd9a7b1/2-KFH%20Disclosure%20August-01-2024.pdf) (VERIFIED)
- Kuwait: the CMA's position is that firms licensed abroad still need CMA approval before marketing locally. [Arab Times](https://www.arabtimesonline.com/news/cma-cracks-down-on-misuse-of-name-and-logo-submits-10-reports-to-prosecutors/) (VERIFIED)
- Bahrain: Article 42 of the CBB Law lets the CBB restrict marketing by unauthorised entities, and Decree-Law 37/2025 toughened penalties. [GDN](https://www.gdnonline.com/Details/1392011) (VERIFIED)
- Oman: the FSA warns against unlicensed platforms that promote shares on regional or global exchanges via social media. [Muscat Daily](https://www.muscatdaily.com/2025/03/17/fsa-warns-against-unlicensed-online-platforms/) (VERIFIED)
- Qatar: the QCB will take legal measures against unlicensed providers advertising on social platforms. [The Peninsula](https://thepeninsulaqatar.com/article/22/05/2022/qcb-warns-against-dealing-with-unlicensed-financial-institutions-and-service-providers) (VERIFIED)

### Inferences
- INFERENCE: A non-product "brand" ad (for example "SELEQT: Indian tax and estate planning for NRIs") is still promotion of advisory services. In most GCC states that is close to, or within, the regulated perimeter for investment advice. Wording and audience need local counsel review.
- INFERENCE: The local publication or radio station may carry its own exposure (UAE media content standards, Kuwait promoter licence), so Gulf outlets may ask for a local licence before accepting financial ads.

### Gaps
- I found no explicit rule on whether advisory services under foreign tax law (as opposed to securities) need a local financial licence in each GCC state.

## Do contributors to community newsletters or radio shows need any licence?

### Takeaway
An unpaid guest contributor who does not promote products generally has no individual media licence requirement in the sources found; the outlet holds the licence. The exceptions are paid or self-promotional advertising content (UAE Advertiser Permit, Saudi Mawthooq for paid promotion, Kuwait promoter licence from April 2027, Oman MoCIIP social media marketing licence) and anyone giving financial recommendations to UAE audiences (CMA finfluencer registration, which reportedly covers seminars and TV).

### Cited Findings
- UAE: the Advertiser Permit has applied to online advertisers since 1 February 2026. [Middle East Briefing](https://www.middleeastbriefing.com/news/uae-influencers-must-obtain-advertiser-permit-under-new-media-law/) (VERIFIED)
- UAE: the finfluencer regime covers seminars and TV appearances. [Hadef](https://hadefpartners.com/news-insights/insights/regulating-influence-the-uae-s-new-compliance-regime-for-finfluencers/) (VERIFIED, secondary)
- Oman: media activity licences come from the Ministry of Information, with social media licences reportedly limited to Omanis. [GC4HR](https://www.gc4hr.org/executive-regulations-for-media-issued-as-part-of-systematic-pattern-of-repression-conducted-by-authorities/). Social media marketing needs a MoCIIP licence. [Times of Oman](https://timesofoman.com/article/139422-omans-ministry-warns-against-unlicensed-marketing-on-social-media) (VERIFIED)
- Kuwait: the promoter licence applies to citizens, expats and visitors targeting Kuwaiti audiences, including unpaid promotion, from April 2027. [Kuwait Times](https://kuwaittimes.com/article/50960/kuwait/other-news/what-kuwaits-new-media-law-will-mean-for-influencers/) (VERIFIED)
- Bahrain: there is no specific influencer law. [GDN](https://www.gdnonline.com/Details/1408389/Clear-rules-urged-to-help-content-creators-sector) (VERIFIED)
- Qatar: influencer licensing is only a proposal. [Qatar Law](https://qatarlaw.com/news/state-licensing-framework-proposed-for-digital-content-creators-in-qatar) (VERIFIED)

### Inferences
- INFERENCE: SELEQT staff appearing as guest experts on UAE radio or at seminars should avoid specific buy, sell or hold recommendations on securities or funds. Otherwise UAE CMA registration and, for SELEQT's own content, the Advertiser Permit questions arise.

### Gaps
- No source addressed print-only community newsletters (as opposed to social media) for contributor licensing in any GCC state.

## Any enforcement examples against foreign advisers using local media or influencers?

### Takeaway
Enforcement found is mostly public warnings plus a few sanctions. Examples are UAE SCA/CMA warnings naming unregistered finfluencers and foreign-linked firms (2025-2026), a Saudi CMA SAR 250,000 order against an unlicensed Telegram/X adviser (2025), a Kuwait CMA disciplinary resolution for marketing foreign fund units without a licence (2024), and regional regulators joining the June 2025 and April 2026 international weeks of action against illegal finfluencers. I found no case involving an Indian SEBI-registered firm advertising in Gulf media.

### Cited Findings
- UAE, 30 June 2025: the SCA warned against an unregistered finfluencer (Instagram/YouTube) and against MEX Atlantic Corporation as unlicensed. The list of licensed finfluencers is published under "Open Data". [Khaleej Times](https://www.khaleejtimes.com/business/uae-sca-warning-unlicensed-finfluencer-companies) (VERIFIED)
- UAE (date not visible): the SCA warned against Top Profit Marketing Services and an Instagram influencer, "borajb", who is not a registered finfluencer. [Gulf News](https://gulfnews.com/business/markets/sca-warns-investors-against-unlicensed-top-profit-marketing-firm-instagram-influencer-borajb-1.500272946) (VERIFIED)
- UAE, August 2026: the CMA warned against Zetta Capital, an unlicensed firm. [Khaleej Times](https://www.khaleejtimes.com/uae/uae-warning-unlicensed-firm-zetta-capital-financial-activities) (VERIFIED)
- Saudi Arabia, 2025: an unnamed unlicensed adviser was ordered to pay SAR 250,000 for paid advice via Telegram promoted on X (October 2023 to July 2024), under CML Article 31 and SBR Articles 5 and 17. [Zawya](https://www.zawya.com/en/capital-markets/equities/saudi-cma-imposes-fine-on-unlicensed-investment-advisor-olbh72pj); [MENA Fintech](https://mena-fintech.org/news/saudi-cma-imposes-fine-on-unlicensed-investment-advisor/) (VERIFIED)
- Kuwait, 2024: CMA disciplinary resolution for marketing foreign CIS units without a licence. [KFH disclosure](https://kfh.com/dam/jcr:22ff3876-71b0-4185-bd28-68214dd9a7b1/2-KFH%20Disclosure%20August-01-2024.pdf). About 10 prosecutor referrals over social ads falsely claiming CMA affiliation. - International: the UAE took part in the June 2025 week of action against illegal finfluencers. [FCA](https://www.fca.org.uk/news/press-releases/fca-leads-international-crackdown-illegal-finfluencers/printable/print). The QFMA and QFCRA took part in April 2026. [QNA](https://qna.org.qa/en/News-Area/News/2026-4/23/qfma-and-qfcra-participate-in-international-week-of-action) (VERIFIED)

### Inferences
- INFERENCE: The typical first sanction for a foreign firm is a public warning naming it, which is a serious reputational risk for an NRI-facing wealth brand. Fines and prosecution have so far targeted firms that actually took money or gave paid advice.

### Gaps
- No enforcement found against Indian advisers, SEBI RIAs or MFDs in Gulf media, and none involving print or radio (as opposed to social media).
