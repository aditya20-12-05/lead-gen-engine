# Gulf Gujaratis gather in Samajs, not newsrooms

There is **no Gujarati-language newspaper, magazine, radio station, radio programme, TV channel or news site based in any of the six Gulf states**, today or historically, that research could find. A 2024 academic study reported by Gulf Today states outright that the UAE has no Gujarati ethnic newspaper even though Gujarati speakers are numerous ([Gulf Today](https://www.gulftoday.ae/culture/2024/10/10/pioneering-study-on-malayalam-newspapers-in-the-uae)), and parallel searches in English and Gujarati script turned up nothing for Qatar, Oman, Bahrain, Kuwait or Saudi Arabia. What does exist is a community layer: Gujarati Samaj UAE (a monthly newsletter, an app, WhatsApp groups, and the Vyapar Junction business network), Gujarati Samaj Qatar, Muscat Gujarati Samaj, Shree Gujarati Samaj Bahrain, and informal Facebook and WhatsApp groups in Saudi Arabia, with nothing verifiable in Kuwait. Around it sit Hindi FM stations, English dailies whose community pages cover Samaj events, and India-based Gujarati TV channels, e-papers and Chitralekha, which Gulf Gujaratis consume digitally. For SELEQT Private Wealth the realistic routes are therefore an invited educational talk at a Samaj event, unpaid guest content on Samaj channels, and Gujarati columns published in India. Paid ads in Gulf media are the one route the rules nearly close: every GCC state treats promoting financial services to its residents as a licensed activity, judged by where the audience sits. On the Indian side, any SELEQT-branded piece is likely an advertisement needing prior approval from BASL under the SEBI IA code, and MFD material must follow AMFI's disclosure rules. SEBI's proposed Common Advertisement Code would exempt pure education, but its final circular had not been confirmed as of October 2026.

**How to read this report.** Every page fetch failed in the research environment, so every finding rests on search-result snippets or search-engine summaries of the cited URL; no page was read in full. **VERIFIED** means a snippet tied the claim to the cited URL. **INFERENCE** means the researchers' or this writer's reasoning. An absence finding means "not found in indexed search", which is strong evidence but not proof. Audience figures are mostly **self-reported** by the outlet or association and are flagged as such, and conflicts between sources are shown rather than resolved. Primary statute texts were not read. Nothing here is legal advice, and every Gulf conclusion needs local counsel before SELEQT acts.

## Six countries, zero Gujarati presses, and only Malayalam and Tamil print

The absence is consistent across every format. In the UAE, the only Indian-language papers printed locally are Malayalam titles (Malayala Manorama, Gulf Madhyamam, Siraj, Mathrubhumi) and Tamil's Daily Thanthi. The KT Abdurabb study notes "the absence of similar ethnic newspapers in other Indian languages such as Hindi, Telugu, Marathi or Gujarati" (VERIFIED, [Gulf Today](https://www.gulftoday.ae/culture/2024/10/10/pioneering-study-on-malayalam-newspapers-in-the-uae)), and Wikipedia's list of UAE newspapers carries no Gujarati title (VERIFIED, [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_newspapers_in_the_United_Arab_Emirates)). The one regional-language print model in the Gulf, a Dubai edition of an Indian daily, is the one Gujarati papers have not followed (VERIFIED, [Khaleej Times](https://www.khaleejtimes.com/uae/leading-tamil-newspaper-now-available-in-uae?amp=1)). One aggregator lists a "Gulf Gujarati" daily news portal in Dubai, but a direct search found no such site, so treat it as a probable spurious directory entry (UNVERIFIED, [a1omads list](https://www.a1omads.com/newspaperslist/languages/gujarati/)).

Radio tells the same story. UAE South Asian FM runs in Hindi and Urdu (Hum 106.2, Radio Mirchi, City 101.6, Radio 4 89.1), Malayalam and Tamil, and Wikipedia's inventory of Dubai radio languages lists eighteen languages without Gujarati (VERIFIED, [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radio_and_television_channels_of_Dubai)). The National describes the market as "English, Hindi, Urdu, Malayalam and Tagalog stations" competing with Arabic (VERIFIED, [The National](https://thenationalnews.com/uae/gold-adds-to-uae-s-wealth-of-radio-stations-1.511933)). One directory tags City 101.6 with Gujarati among its languages, which does not prove a Gujarati show exists (VERIFIED as a listing only, [OnlineRadioBox](https://onlineradiobox.com/ae/city1016/)). Station schedules could not be fetched, so a seasonal Gujarati segment cannot be fully ruled out, but none is indexed. Qatar's South Asian stations are Hindi (Radio Olive 106.3, One FM 89.6) and Malayalam ([Qatar Tribune](https://www.qatar-tribune.com/article/93696/NATION/Culture-minister-launches-four-new-Hindi-Malayalam-radio-stations)). Oman appears to have no licensed terrestrial Indian FM station at all, per a dated user forum ([Expat.com](https://www.expat.com/en/forum/middle-east/oman/muscat/500030-indian-fm-in-muscat.html)). Bahrain has Radio Mirchi in Hindi and Malayalam ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radio_in_Bahrain)), Kuwait has had a weekly Hindi half-hour on state radio since April 2024 ([Kuwait Times](https://kuwaittimes.com/article/13440/kuwait/other-news/radio-kuwait-launches-first-hindi-broadcast)), and Saudi Arabia licensed a Hindi FM station that was planned for 2023 but whose launch is unconfirmed ([Madhyamam](https://madhyamamonline.com/amp/middle-east/saudi-arabia/cluster-arabia-group-set-to-launch-first-foreign-language-fm-radio-stations-in-saudi-arabia-1142061)).

History does not change the picture. Muscat's Gujarati merchant families, present in Muttrah, Sur and Muscat since the late eighteenth century, left account books and letters, not a press: the Indian Embassy in Muscat and the National Archives of India digitised over 7,000 documents in English, Arabic, Gujarati and Hindi from 32 Gujarat-origin families in May 2024 (VERIFIED, [DeshGujarat](https://deshgujarat.com/2024/05/28/historical-documents-of-32-gujarat-origin-families-digitized-in-muscat/)). Kuwait Times' historic South Asian sections were in Malayalam and Urdu, never Gujarati (VERIFIED, [Kuwait Times](https://kuwaittimes.com/article/50337/kuwait/other-news/a-multilingual-legacy/)). Pre-internet archives and library catalogues were not searched, so a cyclostyled Samaj bulletin from the 1970s or 1980s remains possible but undocumented.

**Gujarati Samaj UAE is the only confirmed Gulf Gujarati periodical publisher.** Its site says it publishes event announcements, member achievements, business stories, newcomer guides and "a monthly community newsletter" (VERIFIED, [gujaratisamajuae.ae](https://gujaratisamajuae.ae/)), and its contact page says members stay connected "through our newsletter, social media channels, and WhatsApp groups" (VERIFIED, [contact page](https://www.gujaratisamajinuae.com/contact-us/)). Founded in 2022 with 16 members by Priteesh Patel, chairman of Akshar Holdings, it was the subject, with Vyapar Junction, of an August 2026 press release carried by Zawya, Observer Dubai, Dubai City Guide and UpperNews (VERIFIED, [Zawya](https://www.zawya.com/en/press-release/companies-news/300000-strong-gujarati-community-in-the-uae-gears-up-to-boost-3.4bln-uae-investment-into-gujarat-economy-421927); [Observer Dubai](https://observerdubai.com/300000-strong-gujarati-community-in-the-uae-gears-up-to-boost-us34-billion-uae-investment-into-gujarat-economy/)). It co-powered a Dandiya Raas at Mamzar Park on 10 October 2026 (VERIFIED, [Biz Today](https://www.biztoday.news/2026/10/06/navratri-bazaar-celebrates-fashion-culture-and-community-in-dubai-announces-dandiya-raas-2026/)). Three cautions apply. The **28,000-member figure is self-reported** and unaudited; the same release puts the UAE Gujarati population at 300,000 with about 60% business owners, a community estimate rather than Samaj membership. INFERENCE: a Facebook page of about 1,023 likes sits awkwardly beside 28,000 members, so the figure should be treated as an upper bound until SELEQT sees the newsletter's actual distribution list. The app listing is VERIFIED as published, yet the press release called it "forthcoming" and a later search did not surface it, so its launch status is unclear ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/ng/app/gujarati-samaj-uae/id6795800415)). And no newsletter archive, editor, circulation figure, rate card or policy on outside columns was found. INFERENCE: its "business stories" and "newcomer guides" mix makes it the most plausible home for an NRI finance column anywhere in the Gulf.

Two name clashes need care. An older "Gujarati Samaj" in Dubai launched a website in 2005 (VERIFIED, [Khaleej Times](https://www.khaleejtimes.com/uae/gujarati-samaj-launches-its-web-site)) and a separate "Gujarati Samaj Dubai" Facebook page exists ([Facebook](https://www.facebook.com/GujaratiSamaj.Dubai)); INFERENCE: these are probably distinct from the 2022 body. Gulf Gujaratis read and watch Gujarati in Gujarati only through India-based outlets, and none publishes a Gulf-specific audience figure, edition or bureau. Carriage of TV9 Gujarati, ABP Asmita, GSTV, Zee 24 Kalak and VTV on Gulf IPTV is unverified; Qatar's Ooredoo showed no Gujarati listing.

## Gulf rules test the audience, and SEBI tests the byline

Two rule sets stack on every SELEQT appearance. The Gulf side asks whether SELEQT is promoting financial services or giving recommendations to residents, and in the UAE and, from 2027, Kuwait, whether it is advertising to local audiences under media law. The Indian side asks whether the piece is an advertisement by an IA or a distributor.

The UAE regime's reach is the biggest open question. Hadef & Partners says the finfluencer regime covers **public speaking events, seminars and TV appearances** and applies wherever the creator sits if the audience is in the UAE, while a Khaleej Times report says it applies only to UAE-based influencers, and Clyde & Co says content published from DIFC or ADGM is not directly caught (VERIFIED, conflicting, [Hadef](https://hadefpartners.com/news-insights/insights/regulating-influence-the-uae-s-new-compliance-regime-for-finfluencers/); [Khaleej Times](https://www.khaleejtimes.com/business/uae-sca-warning-unlicensed-finfluencer-companies); [Clyde & Co](https://www.clydeco.com/en/insights/2026/09/the-uae-s-finfluencer-regime)). Commentators treat the Media Council permit and CMA finfluencer registration as cumulative. Registration terms also conflict: fees are reported as waived for three years or AED 5,000 a year, and eligibility as a CFA or CMA-accredited qualification with six months' experience, plus a 1,000-follower threshold that only one source mentions ([UAE Expert Hub](https://www.uaeexperthub.com/uae-finfluencer-license-cma-registration/)). The Advertiser Permit's reported exemptions (own product on a personal account backed by a UAE trade licence, and non-commercial education by under-18s) do not fit SELEQT ([Gulf News](https://gulfnews.com/uae/new-uae-law-advertiser-permit-now-mandatory-for-influencers-and-creators-for-social-media-1.500427938)). Enforcement so far is mostly public naming, such as the June 2025 SCA warnings against an unregistered finfluencer and MEX Atlantic and the August 2026 CMA warning on Zetta Capital ([Khaleej Times](https://www.khaleejtimes.com/uae/uae-warning-unlicensed-firm-zetta-capital-financial-activities)), plus a Saudi CMA order of SAR 250,000 against an unlicensed adviser selling tips via Telegram and X ([Zawya](https://www.zawya.com/en/capital-markets/equities/saudi-cma-imposes-fine-on-unlicensed-investment-advisor-olbh72pj)). No action against a SEBI-registered Indian firm in Gulf media was found. The sharpest nearby precedent is the DFSA's 1 October 2026 fine of AED 401,000 on Vault Wealth for inviting prospects to an unregulated DIFC office for advice and collecting KYC documents, with the DFSA stating that another regulator's licence does not permit services in or from DIFC ([WAM](https://www.wam.ae/en/article/c2ivgxq-dfsa-fines-vault-wealth-limited-109200-for)). INFERENCE: for an NRI-facing wealth brand, being publicly named is the realistic first sanction, and it is reputationally severe.

### SEBI and AMFI treat a branded byline as an advertisement

SEBI registration is what lets SELEQT advise NRIs at all, since the IA Regulations' exemption for overseas clients excludes NRIs and PIOs ([Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/markets/news/person-giving-advice-to-overseas-clients-exempted-from-registration-sebi-123080800347_1.html)). It authorises nothing in the Gulf. The **IA Advertisement Code** (circular of 5 April 2023, effective 1 May 2023) covers every communication by or for an IA that could influence investment decisions, requires prior approval from BASL, and bans superlatives such as "best", "No. 1" and "top" (VERIFIED, [ARIA copy of circular](https://aria.org.in/beta/wp-content/uploads/2024/10/SEBI-Advertisement-code-for-Investment-Advisers-IA-and-Research-Analysts-RA1.pdf); [Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/markets/news/sebi-crackdowns-on-misleading-investment-ads-bars-superlative-terms-123040501056_1.html)). It also requires the registered name, address, SEBI number, logo and standard risk warning, translated accurately if the piece is not in English, which directly affects any Gujarati column ([Taxmann](https://www.taxmann.com/post/blog/sebi-issues-advertisement-code-for-investment-advisers-research-analysts/)). INFERENCE: a SELEQT column or talk slide carrying the firm's name, SEBI number or a call to action counts as an advertisement needing prior approval, wherever it runs. The change on the horizon is SEBI's **Common Advertisement Code**: the 23 June 2026 consultation proposes reporting within 24 hours of issue instead of prior approval, and commentators say purely educational content that does not promote the entity's products would fall outside it ([SEBI consultation](https://www.sebi.gov.in/sebi_data/attachdocs/jun-2026/1782210871344.pdf); [Vinod Kothari](https://vinodkothari.com/2026/06/sebis-new-advertisement-code-dil-khol-ke-advertise-kar/)). One report dated 25 September 2026 says the SEBI Board approved it ([RetailIntel](https://retailintel.in/signal/sebi-clears-common-advertising-code-eases-approval-norms-for-0dfe2a20)), but no final circular or effective date was found, so **prior approval remains the operative assumption**.

Three further Indian constraints shape the routes. The **association ban** gazetted on 29 August 2024 bars regulated entities from direct or indirect links with anyone giving unregistered securities advice or making unauthorised return claims; the 29 January 2025 clarification permits pure investor-education entities that give no advice, make no claims and use price data at least three months old ([KS&K](https://ksandk.com/newsletter/sebi-unregistered-investment-claim-makers/)). INFERENCE: paying a Gulf Gujarati influencer account such as one advertising "brand collaborations" for a SELEQT post needs a check against this ban, as well as the UAE Advertiser Permit. On the MFD side, AMFI told distributors in December 2025 to follow its advertising guidelines, use the SEBI standard risk warning verbatim and avoid celebrities without SEBI approval, and registered name and ARN have been required on social media since 1 May 2026 ([Cafemutual](https://cafemutual.com/news/industry/36413-amfi-tells-mfds-not-to-publish-misleading-posts); [News on AIR](https://www.newsonair.gov.in/sebi-mandates-registered-name-number-disclosure-on-social-media)). SEBI also requires identity verification before intermediaries advertise on social media, and Meta has required a SEBI registration number since July 2025 ([Mondaq](https://webiis10.mondaq.com/india/securities/1759228/sebis-new-digital-compliance-rules-what-investment-advisers-must-know-in-2026)). Finally, the 2020 segregation rule lets a non-individual RIA run distribution only through a separate entity or division with client segregation at family level, so each appearance must be attributed to either the RIA or the MFD side, never both ([Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/amp/article/finance/under-new-ria-norms-all-your-financial-needs-won-t-be-under-one-roof-120070901037_1.html)). SEBI's Baap of Chart order also held that an "educational purposes only" label did not cure what was in substance advice, so the content must be educational in fact, not only in its disclaimer ([SEBI order](https://www.sebi.gov.in/sebi_data/attachdocs/dec-2024/1733151002336_1.pdf)).

## Three workable routes, each with its own rule set

The routes rank by how far they keep SELEQT out of Gulf promotion rules while still reaching Gulf Gujaratis. There is one precedent for each kind: Shalby Multispeciality Hospital of Ahmedabad gave a "Health Talk at Gujarati Samaj Muscat" (VERIFIED, date not seen, [Shalby](https://www.shalby.org/health-talk-gujarati-samaj-muscat/)), MGS has hosted invited Gujarati literary figures for Gujarat Day ([MENAFN](https://menafn.com/1096892493/Oman-Muscat-Gujarati-Samaj-celebrates-58th-Gujarat-Day-with-pride)), and Rajkot's Ashutosh Financial Services ran a webinar "exclusively for UAE and other Gulf-country NRIs" in 2020 and published the Q&A as a web page ([Ashutosh](https://ashutoshfinserv.com/nri-services/burning-questions-of-uae-other-gulf-countries-non-resident-indians-nris/)).

**The India-published column is the cleanest route.** It speaks to Gulf Gujaratis through the e-papers they already read, keeps SELEQT on home ground and engages SEBI rules SELEQT already works under. A Gulf-specific angle (NRE and NRO accounts, FEMA, repatriation, Indian tax for UAE residents) fills a gap the papers' US and UK-leaning NRI sections leave open. INFERENCE: as long as the piece names no product, quotes no returns and invites no one to buy, the Gulf finfluencer and promotion definitions are hard to trigger, though the UAE's audience-based reading means none of this is a clean safe harbour.

**Samaj guest content works if SELEQT stays unpaid and uninvolved in promotion.** An unpaid contributor who does not promote products generally needs no individual media licence in the sources found, because the outlet holds it. INFERENCE: the line moves once the piece carries SELEQT's logo, contact details or a call to action, which is exactly what the SEBI code requires an advertisement to carry. In practice SELEQT should either run Samaj content as clearly bylined, BASL-approved education with no sign-up prompt aimed at Gulf residents, or keep its Gulf-channel pieces purely educational and route interested readers to India-hosted material. The Gujarati Samaj UAE newsletter is the obvious first approach, by its published email, given that the Samaj's stated focus is trade and investment between the UAE and Gujarat.

**A Samaj talk is the highest-value route and the one that most needs counsel.** In person in Dubai, it sits squarely in the disputed reach of the UAE finfluencer regime, and any one-to-one follow-up, advice or document collection at the venue moves SELEQT toward the Vault Wealth facts. INFERENCE: an online session hosted from Ahmedabad for Samaj members, booked by the Samaj and open to self-registration, keeps the content educational and the advice relationship in India. In Qatar the approach should run through GSQ's published email, recognising its ICC and embassy umbrella. In Oman it should go through the Indian Social Club, whose Gujarati wing hosted the Shalby talk. Bahrain's Samaj has no captured contact beyond Facebook. Saudi Arabia and Kuwait offer no formal host, and Kuwait's unpaid-promotion rule from 2027 argues for restraint there.

## Conclusion

The negative finding is itself the strategic insight. Because no Gujarati newsroom exists in the Gulf, there is no editor to pitch, no rate card to buy and no Gulf-licensed Gujarati medium whose licence SELEQT could shelter under. Every Gujarati word that reaches Gulf readers either originates in India or travels through a volunteer-run Samaj. That makes the Samajs gatekeepers of trust rather than media businesses, and it moves the regulatory weight onto SELEQT's own conduct: the same talk is low-risk as an Ahmedabad-hosted webinar and materially riskier as a Dubai seminar with a sign-up desk. The open space is real. No Gujarati-language, Gulf-focused NRI finance voice surfaced in print, radio or creator channels, and the India-based papers' NRI pages look west.

Two pending decisions will reshape this map within months, and SELEQT should wait for both before committing spend. First, if SEBI's Common Advertisement Code is finalised as proposed, pure education drops out of prior approval in India, which removes the main friction on the column and Samaj-content routes. Second, Kuwait's executive regulations and any clarification of the UAE finfluencer regime's territorial reach will decide whether invited talks remain viable in person or should stay online. Until then, the defensible posture is Gujarati education published from India, delivered to Samaj audiences by invitation, and kept free of products, returns and Gulf-side onboarding.
