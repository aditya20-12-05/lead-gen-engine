# Tax education, not offshore licences, wins NRI clients

Ashutosh Financial Services (Rajkot) and International Money Matters (Bengaluru) both built sizeable NRI books without any overseas licence, entity or office that research could find. They did it from India, by teaching NRIs about Indian tax, FEMA, repatriation and succession and then converting that trust into advisory and distribution relationships. Ashutosh, the closest analogue to SELEQT because it is a Gujarat family practice with a multi-generation legacy, runs a recurring "NRI Financial Conclave" (Season 8 in 2024), country-specific "Financial Awareness Presentations and Interactions" for NRIs in the UK, Kenya, Uganda, Singapore, Australia and the Gulf, Gujarati-language sessions, a large tax and FEMA blog and a dedicated NRI desk, and it self-reports **over 500 NRI clients across 20+ countries**. IMMPL leans on founder authority (Lovaii Navlakhi, an early Indian CFP linked to ARIA), "life transition" content for people relocating abroad or returning to India, yearly visits to NRI clients abroad (as of about 2010) and tie-ups with foreign financial planners; its last hard figure is **about 100 NRIs among about 500 fee-paying clients in 2016**. Larger peers take a different route: 360 ONE, Nuvama and ASK hold DIFC licences and hire bankers in Dubai and Singapore, Anand Rathi runs a Dubai representative office plus an FCA-authorised UK subsidiary, and fintechs such as Belong and SBNRI scale through SEO, cash referrals, Gulf PR and GIFT City USD products. No regulatory action against either firm, or against any Indian wealth firm abroad for NRI marketing, surfaced, but the DFSA's October 2026 fine on Vault Wealth for advising and collecting KYC in DIFC without authorisation marks the red line. For SELEQT the lesson is that the Ashutosh model can be copied almost line for line at close to zero cost, provided every touchpoint is inbound, India-hosted, SEBI ad-code compliant and routed to either the RIA or the MFD side, never both.

**How to read this report.** All research came from search-result snippets and search-engine summaries, because direct page fetches were blocked in this environment; no source page was read in full. Statements below are tagged *verified* (a snippet at the cited URL supported it) or *inference* (the researchers' or this writer's reasoning). Nearly every scale figure for both firms is **self-reported** on their own websites or directories and was not checked against SEBI, AMFI, IRDAI or APMI registers, which could not be reached. Conflicting figures are flagged where they occur. Nothing here is legal advice, and every host-country conclusion needs counsel before action.

## Host-country rules, not SEBI, set the outer boundary

SEBI's IA Regulations exempt advice to clients based outside India **except NRIs and PIOs**, so advising NRIs requires SEBI RIA registration, which SELEQT holds ([Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/markets/news/person-giving-advice-to-overseas-clients-exempted-from-registration-sebi-123080800347_1.html)). That licence governs SELEQT; it does not authorise anything in the client's country. The UAE is the clearest case. The onshore regulator SCA was **renamed the Capital Market Authority (CMA) from 1 January 2026**, with DIFC and ADGM outside its remit ([Regulation Tomorrow](https://www.regulationtomorrow.com/2026/01/uae-sca-renamed-cma-and-other-landmark-legislations/)). Promoting financial products to UAE persons needs an onshore licence, and after the 2023 amendments **reverse solicitation is available only to promoters outside the UAE acting cross-border**, while retail promotion of foreign funds on the mainland has been closed ([Norton Rose Fulbright](https://www.nortonrosefulbright.com/en/knowledge/publications/bbb77961/sweeping-changes-to-the-sca-rules-on-the-distribution-of-foreign-funds-in-the-uae); [Kayrouz](https://www.kayrouzandassociates.com/insights/foreign-fund-promotion-uae)). Since 28 May 2025 the UAE has licensed "finfluencers", and the regime reaches investment recommendations via social media, podcasts, webinars and **in-person seminars** ([Pinsent Masons](https://www.pinsentmasons.com/out-law/news/uae-new-licensing-regime-finfluencers); [Khaleej Times](https://www.khaleejtimes.com/uae-finfluencer-licence-sca)). A general free-zone "consultancy" licence such as DMCC is non-regulated and does not cover advice ([Global Law Experts](https://globallawexperts.com/how-to-set-up-an-investment-advisory-firm-in-uae-2026/)). Even a DIFC licence stops at the DIFC boundary: Nuvama told analysts DIFC limits it to "a certain set of clients" and that it was pursuing an onshore licence to serve diaspora clients ([Nuvama BSE filing](https://www.bseindia.com/xml-data/corpfiling/AttachHis/59614158-b987-4da3-b53c-9d8e4bdb3c02.pdf)).

Other jurisdictions are no softer. In Singapore, acting as a financial adviser needs an MAS licence with a resident CEO of 10+ years' experience and at least three experienced local staff ([MAS](https://www.mas.gov.sg/regulation/capital-markets/apply-for-licensing-or-registration-of-capital-market-entities/financial-advisers)). The UK FCA treats any promotion "capable of having effect in the UK" as in scope and, in the HTX case, said a terms-and-conditions exclusion does not protect a site UK users can still reach ([CMS](https://cms.law/en/gbr/legal-updates/global-cryptoexchange-sued-by-uk-regulator-for-breaching-financial-promotions-rules)); one useful carve-out, FPO Article 31, lets an overseas firm send non-real-time communications to a "previously overseas customer" it dealt with in the last 12 months while that person lived outside the UK ([legislation.gov.uk](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2005/1529/article/31/made)). ESMA has said "I agree" boxes claiming client initiative are not reverse solicitation ([ESMA](https://www.esma.europa.eu/sites/default/files/library/esma35-43-2509%5Fstatement%5Fon%5Freverse%5Fsolicitation.pdf)). For North America, FATCA led many AMCs to restrict US and Canada NRIs, and AMC declaration forms require the investor to confirm they transacted **while physically present in India** and that the AMC did not solicit them abroad ([Taurus declaration](https://taurusmutualfund.com/sites/default/files/2025-10/Declaration_from_NRIs_PIOs_Based_in_USA_Canada.pdf)); NRIs in the UK, UAE, Saudi Arabia, Singapore and most of Europe face no AMC-level residency restriction ([Dealplexus](https://www.dealplexus.com/blog/nri-mutual-fund-restrictions-kyc)). US SEC treatment of Indian RIAs serving US residents was not researched successfully, and rules in Kenya, Uganda and Australia, where Ashutosh is active, were not researched at all.

SEBI adds four constraints that shape any outreach. The **IA advertisement code** covers every communication including social media and messaging, requires the registered name, address, SEBI number, logo and a standard risk warning (translated accurately if non-English), and under the 6 February 2026 IA Master Circular **all IA ads need prior approval from the SEBI-recognised supervisory body** ([Taxmann](https://www.taxmann.com/post/blog/sebi-issues-advertisement-code-for-investment-advisers-research-analysts/); [SEBI consultation paper, Jun 2026](https://www.sebi.gov.in/sebi_data/attachdocs/jun-2026/1782210871344.pdf)). The **2020 segregation rule** lets a non-individual RIA run distribution only through a separate entity or division with group-level client segregation, so no client or family gets both ([Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/amp/article/finance/under-new-ria-norms-all-your-financial-needs-won-t-be-under-one-roof-120070901037_1.html)). The **29 August 2024 association ban** bars regulated persons from any direct or indirect link, including money, client referrals or tech integration, with unregistered advisers or anyone making return claims, with a carve-out for pure investor education ([Nishith Desai](https://nishithdesai.com/research-and-articles/hotline/technology-law-analysis/securities-market-regulators-continued-quest-against-unfiltered-financial-advice-15193); sources date the measure inconsistently). And AMFI's code **prohibits rebating** commission to investors ([AMFI unified circular](https://amfiindia.com/themes/theme1/downloads/circulars/unifiedamficircular.pdf)). One opening is moving in SELEQT's favour: SEBI's 14 August 2026 consultation proposes **fully digital KYC for NRIs and OCIs from abroad**, with comments closed 4 September 2026 and adoption not confirmed ([Business Today](https://www.businesstoday.in/nri/story/nris-ocis-can-complete-sebi-kyc-from-abroad-what-changes-under-new-proposal-549339-2026-08-14)).

### Regulatory actions: none against these firms, one sharp precedent

No SEBI, DFSA, CMA, MAS or FCA action naming Ashutosh, IMMPL or any SEBI-registered Indian wealth firm for NRI marketing was found. The precedent that matters is the **DFSA's 1 October 2026 fine of AED 401,000 on Vault Wealth**, an ADGM-licensed firm whose staff worked from a related unregulated office in DIFC in 2024, invited prospects there for advice and collected KYC documents to open accounts; the DFSA said authorisation by another regulator does not permit services in or from DIFC ([WAM](https://www.wam.ae/en/article/c2ivgxq-dfsa-fines-vault-wealth-limited-109200-for); [The National](https://www.thenationalnews.com/business/money/2026/10/01/dfsa-fines-vault-wealth-dh401000-for-providing-unauthorised-transactions/)). The DFSA also fined Vedas International Marketing Management USD 100,000 in November 2024 for unauthorised and misleading promotions ([Emirates 24|7](https://www.emirates247.com/business/corporate/dfsa-fines-company-for-unauthorised-misleading-financial-promotions-2024-11-05-1.735139)); the UAE regulator routinely names unlicensed promoters and finfluencers ([Khaleej Times](https://www.khaleejtimes.com/business/uae-sca-warning-unlicensed-finfluencer-companies)); the FCA warning list includes a Noida-addressed firm "may be targeting people in the UK" ([FCA](https://www.fca.org.uk/news/warnings/momentum-weekend-investing)); and SEBI keeps banning unregistered advisers, holding in Baap of Chart that an "educational purposes only" disclaimer did not cure unregistered advice ([SEBI order Dec 2024](https://www.sebi.gov.in/sebi_data/attachdocs/dec-2024/1733151002336_1.pdf)). *Inference:* the absence of actions against registered Indian firms reflects complaint-driven enforcement and reliance on inbound, educational content, not permission. The fly-in pattern of holding advice meetings or collecting KYC in Dubai is the closest match to the Vault Wealth facts.

## SELEQT can copy the Rajkot model at near-zero cost

Ashutosh is the right template because its advantages are reproducible from Ahmedabad: a multi-generation legacy (Kothari & Co.'s claimed 65 years; Lakhani Investments since 1987), Gujarati-language trust, compliance-led education, and India-only booking. Its weaknesses are also instructive: a late RIA licence, conflicting self-reported numbers, and an expired-or-not PMS code all undermine credibility that SELEQT can protect by publishing only register-checkable claims. The difference is the client: Ashutosh serves 5,000+ families, while SELEQT's **Rs 1 crore lump sum or Rs 1 lakh per month SIP minimum** means the engine must filter for a small, high-net-worth slice. *Inference:* if SELEQT ever licenses in DIFC, note that Category 4 and 3C licences generally restrict firms to professional clients (roughly USD 1 million in net assets), a higher bar than SELEQT's ticket minimum, so a DIFC entity would serve only part of its NRI base.

### Content: compliance-hook education, segmented by country and by life event

The core lesson from both firms is to lead with the NRI's compliance pain, not with products. A SELEQT library modelled on Ashutosh would cover Indian ITR for NRIs, DTAA and foreign tax credit, TRC, NRO to NRE repatriation, FEMA penalties, succession of Indian assets under foreign law and RNOR planning for returners, with country editions for the Gulf, UK, East Africa, Singapore and Australia, and IMMPL's life-event framing layered on top (moving abroad, returning to India, dual-currency income). Each webinar deck becomes a published PDF and blog post, as Ashutosh does, so one effort feeds both events and search. US and Canada content should stay on tax and eligibility, mirroring SBNRI's explicit "excluding U.S.A. & Canada" flags. Every item must carry the ad-code disclosures and clear supervisory-body prior approval before publication, and Gulf-facing material should avoid product recommendations given the UAE finfluencer regime. *Inference:* tax education is the hook, but if SELEQT does not itself file returns, delivering the tax service needs a CA partner (see partners below). Cost: writing and publishing on the existing site and LinkedIn is zero-spend; any supervisory-body approval fee, paid design or video production, or a paid webinar tier needs approval.

### Events: an India-hosted conclave and invited, unpaid speaking slots

Ashutosh's annual conclave and country sessions translate directly into a branded SELEQT NRI event series hosted online from Ahmedabad, with Gujarati sessions as a differentiator and registration open only to people who sign up themselves. IMMPL's peer-teaching suggests a second, lower-risk event stream: speaking to other Indian planners and professional bodies (ICAI chapters, ARIA-type forums), which generates referrals without touching foreign residents at all. Physical Gulf events are where the risk concentrates. Speaking as an invited educator at IBPC Dubai, ICAI Dubai or a Cafemutual-style summit is the pattern peers use, but the UAE finfluencer licence names in-person seminars and the Vault Wealth fine punishes advice meetings and KYC collection on the ground, so counsel must clear any Gulf appearance and no advice or onboarding should happen there. Cost: online sessions on free tools are zero-spend; any paid webinar licence, travel, venue, sponsorship (IMMPL's Mosaic Digital route) or summit fee needs approval, as does a UAE legal opinion.

### Inbound capture: record initiative, route by jurisdiction and by licence

The lead engine should do what neither firm visibly documents. Every form and enquiry channel (an NRI desk email and WhatsApp number, as Ashutosh runs, webinar sign-up, PDF download) should capture country of residence, how the person found SELEQT, and a timestamped first-contact record that shows genuine inbound initiative, since ESMA has said a tick-box alone is not reverse solicitation. Country should drive routing: US and Canada leads get tax-only content and a note that most investment steps happen during an India visit; UK leads are handled as inbound only, with Article 31 relevant to clients who later move to the UK; Gulf leads never receive geo-targeted promotion. Each qualified lead must be flagged as RIA or MFD at intake, with a family-level check, so the segregation rule is enforced by the system rather than by memory. A qualifying question on investable assets screens against the Rs 1 crore or Rs 1 lakh SIP minimum. The engine should also be ready for SEBI's proposed digital NRI KYC, which would remove the need for an India visit. Cost: forms and routing on existing tools are zero-spend; any CRM subscription, WhatsApp Business API or geo-targeted paid ads need approval, and paid ads into the UK or UAE should be ruled out on regulatory grounds regardless.

### Partners: only regulated counterparties, never paid unregistered referrers

Lawful partner models in the notes are narrow. IMMPL's tie-ups with foreign financial planners and Sanctum's partnership approach point to referral relationships with locally licensed advisers who handle the host-country side. AMFI's overseas-distributor registration lets a foreign-regulated distributor earn AMC commission directly, so SELEQT's ARN never pays an unregistered party ([AMFI circular](https://www.amfiindia.com/uploads/Circular_Registration_form_for_Overseas_Distributors_4543289cac.pdf)); a 2013 report put the fee at USD 1,000 for corporates, a partner cost, not SELEQT's. IFSCA's 20 August 2025 interpretive letter reportedly lets a home-regulated distributor with no GIFT presence distribute GIFT products, which opens a reverse model where a Gulf-licensed firm solicits and SELEQT advises from India ([ELP Law](https://elplaw.in/leadership/distribution-of-capital-market-products-in-gift-ifsc-guidance-from-the-ifsca/)). Geojit's JV and bank-desk model is the long-run version. What SELEQT cannot do is pay Gulf community pages, YouTubers or unregistered introducers per lead, copy Belong's cash referral bonus, or rebate commission, given the 2024 association ban, the RIA fee rules and AMFI's anti-rebating code. *Inference:* any revenue share with a foreign partner needs checking against SEBI IA fee rules and the association ban, and an Indian CA partner for tax filing should be structured without referral payments until counsel confirms. Cost: IFSCA distributor registration (fee not found), a DFSA Representative Office (**USD 6,000 application fee**, marketing and introductions only, no advice or arranging) and a DFSA or ADGM Category 4 licence (**base capital quoted at USD 10,000 to 50,000 across conflicting sources**, plus application fees of USD 15,000 or more and substance costs) all need approval and are best deferred until inbound volume justifies them ([DFSA services](https://services.dfsa.ae/authorised-person/authorised-firms-request-for-new-authorisation-representative-offices/); [Zitadelle](https://www.zitadelleag.com/services/investment-licensing/dubai-dfsa-category-4); [Salvus Funds](https://salvusfunds.com/?p=23086)). Whether DFSA accepts a SEBI RIA as a home-regulated firm for a rep office is unconfirmed.

## Conclusion

The evidence reframes what "global outreach" means for a firm of SELEQT's size. Neither Ashutosh nor IMMPL went abroad in any regulated sense; both made NRIs come to them, and their moat is a recurring compliance relationship (an annual tax return, a repatriation question, a succession worry) that brings each NRI back every year and is lawful to teach from India. Inbound-only is therefore not a compliance constraint bolted onto growth but the growth strategy itself, and the firms that did go offshore did so with capital, licences and banker teams that sit at a different scale. For SELEQT the sharper competitive point is that the Rajkot template is visible but imperfectly executed: Ashutosh's late RIA, inconsistent self-reported figures and undisclosed segregation approach leave room for an Ahmedabad firm with a 1987 legacy to compete on verifiable credibility, clean RIA and MFD separation and Gujarati-language depth, while measuring the engine by qualified inbound conversations at the Rs 1 crore threshold rather than by sign-up volume.

Three open questions should be closed before scaling. First, counsel should confirm how UAE, UK, Kenyan, Ugandan and Australian rules treat country-titled educational webinars delivered from India, since the notes cover only the UAE and UK and Ashutosh's public record offers no disclaimer wording to borrow. Second, the supervisory-body prior-approval process should be costed and timed, because it becomes the bottleneck for a content-heavy engine. Third, the claims in this report about Ashutosh and IMMPL should be re-checked on the SEBI, AMFI and APMI registers and the firms' live pages from an unrestricted network before any of it is cited externally.
